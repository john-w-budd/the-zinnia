Dear Local President:

I'm following up on your inquiry about ways to use your upcoming contract
negotiations at The Zinnia to improve diversity, equity, inclusion, and
belonging. This is a critically important area so I am pleased that you
asked for more information. It's a broad area so I can provide some starting
ideas, but many other resources can be found online. Also remember that
social justice is an ongoing issue that needs constant attention. It's not
enough to simply negotiate some new contract language and forget about it.

For starters, you should add a broad non-discrimination clause to your
contract, such as:

Non-discrimination: Neither party will discriminate against employees
or guests on the basis of race, color, creed, national origin, religion, age,
sex, gender identification and gender expression, sexual orientation, marital
status, any real or perceived disability, political affiliation, military
status, status as a protected veteran, use of service animal, genetic
information, status as a victim of domestic violence or sexual assault, or
any other protected class under applicable local, state, or federal law.

To further combat discrimination and harassment, you could craft language that
prohibits the Employer from using non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) in
settlements with workers and guests who have experienced discrimination or
harassment. In this way, the victims' stories can be told, so that problems
can be identified and fixed.

This could be taken further by negotiating a provision for the creation of a
joint diversity committee. Here is one example, but like many other
contractual items, the language can be customized to fit your needs:

Diversity Committee: The Employer shall support and maintain a joint
Diversity Committee  composed of at least four bargaining unit representatives
and no more than four members of management. Bargaining unit representatives
must be members of the bargaining unit will be elected by the members of the
bargaining unit for staggered two-year terms. The purpose of the Diversity
Committee is to work together to improve diversity, equity, inclusion, and
belonging for the Employer's workers and guests. This might involve the
provision of training, involvement in new employee recruitment and
orientation, retention initiatives, and in other activities.

I know you have significant linguistic diversity in your union. A contract
in Denver contains these protections:

Upon request of the employee, the Employer shall provide interpreters from
its staff, where such staff is available, for employees not fluent in English
during any investigative interview that may lead to discipline or discharge.
If readily available, an employee may utilize an interpreter from Hotel
staff of their choosing. Where the Employer is unable to provide an
interpreter, the Union may provide an interpreter. In the event that an
interpreter is not readily available, timelines for issuance of the
disciplinary or discharge notice as set forth in Article 23 shall
automatically be tolled until any appropriate interpreter is available.

The San Francisco and Los Angeles locals have been trying to negotiate on
behalf of undocumented hotel employees.  Both have been successful in
securing a 12-month guarantee of reinstatement with full seniority for
workers whose work authorization expires but who can correct any
immigration problems. Here is additional suggested contract language to
help defend your members:

The Employer will require that any federal immigration agent, Immigration
and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agent, Department of Homeland Security (DHS)
agent, or State and Local law enforcement officials comply with legal
requirements before they may be allowed to interrogate, search or seize the
person or property of any worker. The Employers will refuse entrance to the
workplace or inspection of worker documents without a valid judicial warrant.
Should an ICE or DHS agent demand access to the premises or seek to
interrogate, search, or seize any employee the Employer must contact the
Union immediately.

Your bargaining team can probably identify other areas of needed action,
and you can often find other resources by searching online.

Good Luck in negotiations.

Fraternally Yours,

Director, Civil and Human Rights Department

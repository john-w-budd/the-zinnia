Dear HR Manager:

We are pleased to provide you with our annual analysis of the state of the Twin Cities Hotel Employers-Employees Health and Welfare Trust Fund ("the Fund") to which The Zinnia makes contributions on behalf of its union-represented employees. As you know, the Fund is not a health care provider; rather, the Fund purchases group health insurance on behalf of the participating hotels in the Twin Cities metro area, funded by contributions from the hotels based on hours worked. We work hard to negotiate the best plan rates, but ultimately we are squeezed by the same national trends in health care costs that are affecting all good employers.

Unfortunately, health care costs continue to escalate. The $4.50 per hour contribution per employee that The Zinnia makes will either have to be increased to provide the same level of benefits this year, or employees will have to share an increased portion of the costs. While we are not projecting the double-digit increases of fifteen years ago, we are still forecasting significant increases in costs for the next few years. Nationally, according to the Kaiser Family Foundation, the average total cost of health benefits (including employer and employee contributions) is estimated at $9,325 per employee for individual coverage, and $26,993 for family coverage. Nationally, health benefit costs are expected to increase by 6% in the coming year, which is higher than the 4-5% annual increases in recent years.

Our current plan provides the benefits listed in your collective bargaining agreement at a cost of $7,000 per employee for single coverage which is fully paid by the Fund, and funded by The Zinnia's per-hour contribution. Family coverage costs $20,500; for this coverage, the Fund pays $12,400 and employees pay $8,100 per year. We should expect these rates to increase as much as 6 percent this year if no changes are made in the plan. More generous coverage will of course drive up this increase even more.

The most obvious way to manage employer costs is through increased employee contributions for premiums. We have also calculated two additional measures. One, increasing the annual employee out-of-pocket maximum to maximum allowed by the Affordable Care Act will reduce the annual premium by $1,100 per employee. Two, increasing the prescription drug co-pays by another $15 per drug per month will reduce annual premiums by $950 per employee (single coverage) and $2,500 (family coverage).

For the record, 90% of your bargaining unit employees are currently enrolled in the health insurance plan, and 50% of those enrolled are signed up for family coverage. This is in compliance with the employer mandate under the Affordable Care Act because the Affordable Care Act requires that insurance be offered, not necessarily accepted, by 95% of those who work at least 30 hours per week or 130 hours per month. We offer coverage to 100% of employees who work as little as 20 hours per week.

If you need additional information, please do not hesitate to contact us. Information on employer-sponsored health benefits can also be found on the website of the Kaiser Family Foundation.

Sincerely,

Administrative Director

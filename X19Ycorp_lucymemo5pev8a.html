To:The Zinnia Management Team
From:Lucy Baldercash
Re:Negotiations
I am eagerly awaiting the results of your negotiations with the union. I should not have to remind you that the profits from my hotel have been inadequate as of late--do not make your jobs more difficult by bowing to the union's wishes. Rather, you need to find ways to make the hotel more profitable.
Remember that I persuaded the City of Minnesota to classify The Zinnia as being in a Minneapolis economic opportunity zone. So we're exempt from inflationary increases above the Minneapolis $15 minimum wage law and from the Minneapolis Sick and Safe Time law requiring paid sick leave. But now Minnesota has enacted something similar, and unfortunately you need to make sure The Zinnia complies with the Minnesota Earned Sick and Safe Time requirements in this round of bargaining.
These unfortunate government intrusions into my business magnify your need to find ways to offset these costs. I've been reading about a number of interesting operational innovations you might want to consider. Some hotels have been leasing out space to restaurants. The name recognition draws in customers, but the restaurant is a separate entity and doesn't have to follow the union contract. There are housekeeping apps like Flexkeeping and ALICE Housekeeping that can improve the scheduling and monitoring of our cleaners. This should be a management issue that you can impose without bargaining.
Remember, I'm paying you to identify and implement new ways to increase The Zinnia's profits! If I'm the only one coming up with new ideas, I'll have to find a new management team.
I have also been feeling pressure from my fellow (union) hotel-owners. Their contracts are being renegotiated next year and they feel that the settlement at The Zinnia will set the local pattern. I do not wish to face the wrath of my peers if the pattern is for a large increase in labor costs, and if a union win increases its political clout.

Dear Local President:

As you prepare for bargaining with The Zinnia, here is an update on the public
policy situation:

    

  • Minneapolis Sick and Safe Time Ordinance - The Zinnia is exempt because of
a questionable "special economic opportunity zone" designation. You can
ignore this Minneapolis ordinance in your negotiations.
    • 

  • Minneapolis Minimum Wage Ordinance - The Zinnia must comply with a $15
minimum, but is exempt from mandated inflationary increases because of the
questionable "special economic opportunity zone" designation. This does
not prohibit you from negotiating increases for the jobs that are
traditionally paid at the minimum wage.
    • 

  • Minnesota Paid Leave - This provides paid family and medical leave,
financed by a payroll tax. This is already covered by your collective
bargaining agreement (section 17.3) so you don't need to do anything. But
section 17.3 doesn't prohibit you from negotiating increases in how much
of the payroll tax The Zinnia will cover.
    • 

  • Minnesota Earned Sick and Safe Time (ESST) - The Zinnia must comply
with this state law. This needs to be part of your negotiations and added
as a new provision in the new contract. The simplest way would be for
The Zinnia to offer at least 48 hours of paid sick leave per year to
every employee.

To continue to build your local political influence to strengthen these kinds
of laws, to counter your owner's ability to get special exemptions, and to
help push the labor movement's progressive agenda in Minnesota, I encourage
you to negotiate a "Voluntary Payroll Deduction of Political Contributions"
clause into your collective bargaining agreement. This makes it easier for
workers to support our critical political lobbying. I'll include a sample
clause at the end of this message.

To help with internal organizing in support of a robust union, it's also
recommended that you negotiate the ability to meet with new employees during
their orientation. A sample clause is included at the end of this message.

I also have two pieces of advice to share based on what we've seen in other
hotel negotiations around the country.

1) Tech companies are aggressively pushing housekeeping apps like hotelkit
and ALICE Housekeeping. But these are designed by programmers, not individuals
who understand the difficulties of room cleaners. You probably understand this
already, but for those on the bargaining team who are less familiar with
housekeeping, here's a newspaper clipping to help them out.

Unfortunately, though, the implementation of new technology is likely seen as
a managerial right. So you can try to dissuade the hotel if they raise this at
bargaining, but ultimately you can't stop it. You should try to negotiate a
new clause granting 90-day advance notice of any new technology implementation,
including major upgrades or updates. Also, labor law entitles you to "effects
bargaining" such that The Zinnia has to bargain over the effects of introducing
any technology. So for a housekeeping app, for example, you can insist that the
hotel bargain over changes in how many rooms need to be cleaned per day.

2) You probably already know that some hotel owners have been trying to
circumvent their collective bargaining obligations by subcontracting out
bargaining unit work (for example, cleaning or food preparation) or by leasing
out existing space to restaurants.  To combat this tactic, some type of
subcontracting clause is highly recommended.  Note that this type of clause may
conflict with an existing management rights clause so that clause may need to
be modified, too.


Good Luck in negotiations.

Fraternally Yours,

Director, Legal Department


Sample Clauses:

Voluntary Payroll Deduction of Political Contributions
The Employer agrees to honor authorizations for voluntary payroll deduction
of political contributions, and to forward all contributions on or before
the 20th day of each month for the previous work month to the Local H-56
Political Action Committee.

New Hire Orientation
The Employer shall notify the Union of all new employees hired into the
bargaining unit. The Union shall be afforded 30 minutes of the newly-hired
employee's regular working time during the on-boarding process with the
Employer. This time will be for the purposes of presenting information about
union membership, the collective bargaining agreement, and bargaining unit
representation.

Subcontracting
For the purpose of preserving work and job opportunities for the employees
covered by this Agreement, the Employer agrees that no work or services of
the kind, nature or type covered by, presently performed, or hereafter
assigned to the collective bargaining unit will be subcontracted, transferred,
leased, assigned or conveyed in whole or in part to any other plant, person,
or nonunit employees, unless otherwise provided in this Agreement.  The
Employer may not subcontract work in any classification for the purpose of
avoiding overtime.  The Employer may not subcontract work in any classification
if any employee who normally performs such work is on layoff.
